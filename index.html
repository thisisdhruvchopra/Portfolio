About

I study the question of how long you can keep a sophisticated attacker inside a deception environment before they recognize it as artificial. I work at C3iHub, IIT Kanpur, where I design, deploy, and operate live honeypot infrastructure against real attacker traffic, including industrial and ICS endpoints.

The problem that drives most of my thinking is credibility over time. Generic honeypots get identified within hours. But a high-fidelity environment that mirrors production infrastructure, with real services, real behavioral signatures, and induced vulnerabilities that match what an attacker expects, buys significantly more dwell time. I want to understand exactly what breaks that illusion, and how to build environments that survive active reconnaissance by sophisticated actors.

I came to this through infrastructure. I started at C3iHub in 2024 as a DevOps intern, moved into security engineering, and then into research. That background is why I treat deception environments as systems to engineer and measure, not one-off deployments. My current work extends the ADAPT deception framework into a deception-as-a-service model, so a honeynet and its threat intelligence pipeline can be provisioned on demand instead of being tied to fixed physical hardware.

I'm currently applying to MS/PhD programs in Europe, primarily Germany, to work on this more rigorously. I'm interested in groups working on intrusion deception, adversarial simulation, or threat-informed defense.

Projects

ADAPT Framework Replication

2026 · Technical review submitted for publication

Physical-hardware replication of ADAPT, an adaptive camouflage-based deception framework for trapping APTs (Putrevu et al., ACM 2024). A 6-VM network across two privilege tiers with HTTP/FTP servers and an OpenVPN backbone, covering all three APT attack paths (APT-40, APT-28, Lazarus/APT-38) with MITRE TTP-aligned honey credentials and induced vulnerabilities. I built Chatterbox, a covert app that exfiltrates logs from all 6 VMs to a Kafka broker over an encrypted channel, and wired the Kafka → Elasticsearch → dashboard pipeline for near real-time attack path correlation.

Evaluating it afterwards showed that the framework's core CVEs (CVE-2021-41773, CVE-2022-0847) are fully patched and no longer representative of real APT tradecraft. I documented this in an independent technical review, now submitted for publication.
